Living in Oak Cliff & Bishop Arts, Dallas

Oak Cliff was a separate town until Dallas annexed it in 1903 and keeps its own feel, with historic districts, hills and parks. Bishop Arts is its walkable core of independent shops, restaurants and galleries.

Home typesSingle-family, Bungalow / Craftsman, Mid-century ranch, Townhome / infill
Home ageWide range: early 1900s homes in the historic districts, mid-century ranches, and newer infill near Bishop Arts.
LotsMix of standard city lots and larger hillside lots in areas like Kessler Park, with some tree-heavy, sloped sites.
HOAMost single-family streets have no mandatory HOA; city historic and conservation districts (such as Winnetka Heights) add exterior design rules.
School districtsDallas ISD

Getting around

Downtown is a short drive across the Trinity via I-35E or the Houston Street viaduct, and the Dallas Streetcar links Bishop Arts to Union Station. North job centers mean crossing downtown.

  • Legacy West (Plano) ~33 min 25 mi

Tradeoffs

Nearby

Development watch

Halperin Park, a deck park over I-35E next to the Dallas Zoo, opened its first phase in May 2026, with a second phase planned. Infill housing continues around Bishop Arts.

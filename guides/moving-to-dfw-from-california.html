Moving to DFW from California: the five things that surprise people

No state income tax is real. So are higher property taxes. Here's how the math and the lifestyle actually change.

Published 2026-10-15 · 6 min read · Source: Editorial (DREI). Verify tax specifics with your county appraisal district.

Every month, families relocate to North Texas from California, Illinois, New York and elsewhere. These are the adjustments that come up again and again.

1. No state income tax, but property tax does the work

Texas has no personal state income tax. Local services are funded heavily by property taxes, so combined rates are higher than most Californians are used to. Budget the tax bill as a real monthly cost, not an afterthought.

2. Homestead exemptions matter

Once you own and live in your home, file a homestead exemption with the county appraisal district. It reduces the taxable value and caps how fast your homestead's appraised value can rise each year. Check your appraisal district for current amounts and deadlines.

3. MUDs and PIDs in new construction

Many new communities are financed by Municipal Utility Districts or Public Improvement Districts. These add to your tax rate or appear as a separate assessment. Always compare the total rate by address.

4. The car is the commute

Toll roads such as the Dallas North Tollway, SH 121 and the Bush Turnpike are part of daily life, and DART rail serves only some suburbs. Pick your area by drive time to the places you actually go.

5. You get more house. Decide what you'll do with it.

Many buyers find their budget stretches further here. The best decisions come from deciding in advance what matters more: space, newness, commute or walkability.
